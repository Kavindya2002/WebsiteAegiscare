About our research

AegisCare: intelligent fall monitoring system for elderly care

We are a team of four final year students of the Faculty of Computing at the Sri Lanka Institute of Information Technology (SLIIT). Our research combines wearable sensing, environmental awareness and health monitoring to prevent falls and respond to them.

Group ID R26_IT_051Information TechnologyFaculty of Computing, SLIIT
Background

The problem we set out to solve

Elderly falls are a major healthcare issue because they can cause serious injury.

Most existing systems detect a fall only after it has happened, and the person has to press a button to call for help. Most fall-prevention tools also solve only one piece of the problem: motion, environment or health, never all three together.

Research objective

Develop one system that recognises activities, checks the environment and the person's health, predicts risk early and sends the alert in real time, automatically.

The objective is also the research gap: no single system joins all of these steps.

Research objectives

Four objectives, one per component

Activity recognition

A wearable IoT system that detects falls in elderly people in real time and explains every decision.

Environmental risk mapping

Detect environmental risk (slippery floors, poor lighting, obstacles) through vision and IoT sensor fusion.

PPG fall-risk prediction

Estimate heart rate, blood pressure and fall-risk status from optical vitals sensing.

Intelligent alert classification

Turn every risk signal into the right intervention: voice warning, caregiver alert or emergency escalation.

Methodology

How the pipeline is built

The method follows the data from the sensor on the person to the alert on the caregiver's screen.

View the architecture

  1. Sense

    A wearable BMI160 motion sensor, light, moisture and distance sensors with an optional camera, and a MAX30102 optical sensor capture motion, surroundings and Red and IR pulse signals.

  2. Process at the edge and transmit

    ESP8266 and ESP32 boards calibrate, filter and package the readings. Data is published over MQTT, with WebSocket streaming used for the vitals backend.

  3. Analyse with machine learning

    Component 1 groups motion into 20-sample windows, extracts 52 features and classifies them with a Random Forest. Component 2 filters and classifies hazards. Component 3 turns PPG signals into blood pressure and heart rate.

  4. Fuse into one risk assessment

    Outputs are combined into a single assessment: whether a fall was detected, its type, a risk score and the surrounding context.

  5. Decide the response

    An XGBoost classifier takes nine features and selects a voice alert, a caregiver alert or an emergency alert.

  6. Deliver and adapt

    Alerts, recovery guidance and live dashboards reach caregivers. Sensor feedback and an adaptive response close the loop.
