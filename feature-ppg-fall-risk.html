What this component is

In this component, the MAX30102 sensor captures raw Red and infrared PPG (photoplethysmography) signals directly from the user's fingertip. A trained machine learning model turns these signals into predicted systolic and diastolic blood pressure values.

Data moves through HiveMQ using the MQTT protocol, alongside WebSockets for real-time streaming to the backend. Based on the continuously monitored blood pressure and heart rate, the system analyses the health metrics to predict whether a fall is likely to occur.

How it works

  1. SensingA MAX30102 sensor on the fingertip captures the Red and infrared PPG signals, powered by a rechargeable battery.
  2. ProcessingAn ESP32 collects the sensor data over I²C and packages it as JSON.
  3. TransmissionData is published over MQTT through HiveMQ Cloud (TLS) and streamed to the backend over a WebSocket.
  4. Server functionsThe PPG-to-blood-pressure model estimates SBP and DBP, heart rate is analysed, fall risk is predicted and an alert decision is made.
  5. StorageReadings and events are stored in a Supabase database in the vital_logs and patient_events tables.
  6. AlertThe alert is forwarded over HiveMQ MQTT to the alert-classification component.

The research gap

Connect vital signs to fall risk

Blood pressure and heart rate are usually measured by separate clinical devices at set times, and they are rarely linked to the risk of a fall. A raw optical signal also has to be turned into meaningful vitals before it can inform any decision.

Vitals measured apart from fall riskRaw PPG is not yet a usable health signal

This component closes that loop. It estimates blood pressure from the PPG signal with a trained model, monitors it continuously with heart rate, and uses the values to predict fall risk, so health is part of the same system as motion and environment.
